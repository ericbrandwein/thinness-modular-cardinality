In this section, we prove the following lemma.

\begin{lemma}
    \label{lemma:gap-between-intevalmc-and-vc}
    There exists an infinite class of graphs $G$ such that $\intervalmc(G) = 2^{\vc(G)} + \vc(G)$. 
\end{lemma}

As $\vc(G) \geq \tc(G)$ and $\intervalmc(G) \leq \clustermc(G)$ for every graph $G$, this will in particular prove that the bound in \cref{lemma:twin-cover-hcupn} is tight.

To prove \cref{lemma:gap-between-intevalmc-and-vc}, for every $k \in \N$ with $k \geq 3$, we build a graph $\graph$ of $2^k + k$ vertices with $\intervalmc(\graph) = 2^{\vc(\graph)} + \vc(\graph)$.

The graph $\graph$ is constructed as follows.
Let $X$ be a set of $k$ vertices, and for each subset $X' \subseteq X$, let $v_{X'}$ be a vertex. We define
\begin{align*}
    V(\graph) &\coloneq X \cup \{v_{X'} \mid X' \subseteq X\}, \text{ and}\\
    E(\graph) &\coloneq \{(x, v_{X'}) \mid x \in X'\}.
\end{align*}

In other words, we create a set $X$ of vertices, and create one extra vertex $v_{X'}$ for each possible neighborhood $X'$ inside $X$. Two vertices outside $X$ are never adjacent to each other, and neither are two vertices inside $X$. Observe that this graph has $2^k + k$ vertices, as there are $2^k$ possible subsets $X' \subseteq X$. \Cref{fig:G-k} shows the graph $\graph[3]$.

It is easy to see that the vertex cover number of $\graph$ is exactly $k$.
\begin{lemma}
    \label{lemma:vc-G}
    $\vc(\graph) = k$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
    Note that every edge in $\graph$ is incident to a vertex in $X$, making $X$ a vertex cover of $\graph$. Hence, $\vc(\graph) \leq k$.

    By the definition of vertex cover, every edge in $\graph$ must have an endpoint in every vertex cover of $\graph$. In particular, the matching of size $k$ formed by the edges $(x, v_{\{x\}})$ for each $x \in X$ shows that every vertex cover must have size at least $k$. Thus, we also have $\vc(\graph) \geq k$, which gives the statement of the lemma.
\end{proof}

It remains to determine the $\interval$-modular cardinality of $\graph$.

\begin{lemma}
    \label{lemma:intervalmc-G}
    $\intervalmc(G) = 2^k + k$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
    As $|V(\graph)| = 2^k + k$, this is equivalent to proving that the only $\interval$-modular partition of $\graph$ is the one that has one part per vertex.

    The graph $\graph$ has two connected components: the one containing $v_\emptyset$, and the one $C$ containing all other vertices in $V(\graph)$. Notice that $C$ does not induce an interval graph when $k \geq 3$, as it contains an induced cycle of size four. Moreover, both $\{v_\emptyset\}$ and $C$ are modules of $\graph$. Hence, as shown by \citet{modular-decomposition}, every $\interval$-modular partition of $\graph$ consists of $\{v_\emptyset\}$ and proper submodules of $C$. To finish the proof, we show that all proper submodules of $C$ have size one.

    To reach a contradiction, suppose there exists some proper submodule $M$ of $C$ of size at least two.

    If all vertices in $M$ are outside $X$, there are two vertices in $M$ with different neighborhoods in $X$, contradicting that $M$ is a module. Hence, there exists a vertex $x \in X$ that belongs to $M$. Let $y$ be a vertex of $M$ distinct from $x$. The vertex $v_{\{x\}}$ must also belong to $M$, as otherwise there would be a vertex outside of $M$ that is adjacent to $x$ and not to $y$. This forces every neighbor of $x$ different from $v_{\{x\}}$ to also belong to $M$, as none of them are adjacent to $v_{\{x\}}$. This includes all vertices $v_{X'}$ where $x \in X'$. For each other vertex $x' \in X$, there exists a vertex $v_{X'}$ in $M$ such that $x, x' \in X'$. The vertex $x'$ is not a neighbor of $x$, and thus must also belong to $M$. We can therefore repeat the previous argument with every $x' \in X$ instead of $x$ to show that every vertex of $C$ belongs to $M$, contradicting that $M$ is a proper submodule of $C$.
\end{proof}

Combining \cref{lemma:vc-G,lemma:intervalmc-G}, we obtain that $\intervalmc(\graph) = 2^{\vc(\graph)} + \vc(\graph)$ for every $k \geq 3$. This implies \cref{lemma:gap-between-intevalmc-and-vc}.

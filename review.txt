1. I could not follow you in the running time arguments for Theorem 7. Could you state more explicitly how you traverse the modular decomposition tree and when do you check for the interval graph property.

2. Is the bound given in Lemma 12 optimal or is it possible that there is also a polynomial bound? This question is of particular interest as if the bound can be improved to polynomial, then this would directly imply a polynomial kernel for thinness when parameterized by the twin cover number and vertex cover number. If this is not the case, then your third open problem would definitely need some new algorithmic insights. If it is open whether there is a polynomial bound, then I would suggest to address this directly after Lemma 12 and in the open problem description.

3. Are you using open or closed intervals in your definition of interval representations and simultaneous interval representations? There are some places where you seem to assume that the intervals are open intervals but you never state this explicitly. One place is line 1 of page 15 where you say that the interval intersects the interval (x,y). In my opinion, this only works if the intervals are open. Similarly, in the proof of Lemma 17 you place the interval representation of H in some sub-interval of A. Again, I think this only works directly if the intervals are open since this ensures that this sub-interval is not just the right end-point of A. I am sure that for simultaneous interval representations, it does not matter whether you use open or closed intervals, due to the result of Frankl and Maehara [1]. Nevertheless, you should give a short hint about this when you are defining the simultaneous interval representations.

[1] Peter Frankl and Hiroshi Maehara. Open-interval graphs versus closed-interval graphs. Discrete Mathematics, 63(1):97–100, 1987. https://doi.org/10.1016/0012-365X(87)90156-7
